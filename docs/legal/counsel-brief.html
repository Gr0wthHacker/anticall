Counsel Brief: Open-Source Call Defense Software

Project "anticall" (working name CallShield Network, not trademark-cleared) · github.com/Gr0wthHacker/anticall · Oct 27, 2026 · DRAFT, not legal advice

What it is

Self-hostable, AGPL-3.0 software that (1) screens a user's own inbound calls, (2) lets the user authorize an automated callback to a recent spam caller to identify who is behind the call (company, seller, transfer target), and (3) builds an evidence file and drafts FTC/FCC/carrier complaints for the user to review and file. Pre-alpha; built on synthetic and mock telephony only.

Built-in limits: no auto-redial or bulk dialing; callbacks only to a logged inbound caller with STIR/SHAKEN attestation A, within 72h, 8am–9pm, one user tap each, max 2 per number per 30 days, 5 per user per day, 3 per number across the instance; the callee hears a fixed “automated assistant” disclosure before any question; wrong-number or stop-calling blocklists the number permanently; no voice biometrics; local processing only; the AI has no dial tool; live speech processing, outbound AI, agency submission and community sharing are all off by default; no output ever says a violation is proven or states damages.

Questions we need answered

  1. Callbacks (gates G1–G5, table below). Is a single disclosed, automated return call to a number that called the user lawful (47 USC 227(b) consent theory, state automated-dialer laws, 47 USC 223)? How much exposure arises if an A-attested number was spoofed? What attempt caps and disclosure wording do you require?
  2. Recording and live AI processing. For inbound screening and outbound calls, what consent or notice is required, by state and across state lines, for (a) live speech-to-text, (b) saving audio, (c) saving transcripts, (d) sending audio to a remote AI vendor? Please fill the jurisdiction matrix (all states currently "UNKNOWN").
  3. Developer and operator exposure. What liability attaches to the maintainer who publishes source, a self-hoster, and any future hosted operator? Is the disclaimer wording adequate, and are any statements overclaiming?
  4. Licensing. Confirm AGPL-3.0-only versus alternatives (e.g. Apache-2.0); compatibility of third-party models, voices and code; whether a separate acceptable-use policy can apply to hosted services only.
  5. Evidence and complaints. Are our evidence labels (observed / claimed / inferred / user-attested) and export wording acceptable? Review the filing attestation text, the FCC/FTC/carrier templates, and whether any agency offers an approved submission channel (we currently only do manual handoff).
  6. Privacy. State privacy and consumer-rights obligations for call metadata and stored media; retention defaults (metadata 90d, audio/transcripts 30d); deletion limits and legal holds; the required operator privacy notice.
  7. Community sharing (later). Defamation and false-attribution risk, minimum anonymity standards, takedown process.
  8. Entity, name, insurance. Recommended entity structure, trademark clearance of the name, and insurance.
  9. Provider terms. Do SIP and AI vendors permit automated/AI-voice calling under their terms?
